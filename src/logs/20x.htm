Unintentional Permacomputing

We're visiting family this month where, given enough time sitting at a table, the choices we make are bound to turn up in conversations and be questioned. Something that we often find ourselves having to explain is how what looks like voluntary simplicity was less an intention than something that naturally followed from higher forces of our environment. That "degrowth" that people often think was a choice, really wasn't.

You can decide not to participate in the upgrade cycle, because you can't afford the extra clutter, or can't afford it literally. Then, there's the obvious degrowth by force when the power goes out or the internet goes down, software rugpulls and corporate capture. Suddenly, books and typewriters are a more dependable interface that doesn't need permission to work. It's that stuff that everyone confuses with nostalgia.

But what I'm thinking about is the most interesting kind of degrowth, the kind you don't notice happening at all. You aren't necessarily pursuing an ideology, you're just making your little corner of the world nicer, and along the way adjusting things in near imperceptible ways. A tidy desk, a pleasant interface, a comfortable tool, a few years later you end up somewhere that indicentally looks like degrowth.

If I make a tool more responsive so that I can spend a quiet evening reading, drawing, or making something with it, this is a very different relationship with technology than optimizing the technology for a specific metric. Our kind of degrowth felt natural to do, and it's why we did it.
