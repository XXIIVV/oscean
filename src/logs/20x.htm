Unintentional Permacomputing

We're visiting family, so some of the choices we make are just bound to turn up in conversations and be questioned. People think degrowth a choice, but it rarely is, it's something that follow higher forces than intention. I'm sitting in traffic mulling over that "degrowth" that people often think is our choice, which really isn't.

Sure, you can decide not to participate in the upgrade cycle, because you can't afford the extra clutter, or can't afford it literally. There's the obvious degrowth by force, the internet goes down, the power goes out, software rugpulls. Suddenly, books and typewriters are a more dependable interface that doesn't need permission to work. That stuff that everyone confuses with nostalgia.

But what I'm thinking about is the most interesting kind of degrowth, the kind you don't notice happening at all. You aren't necessarily pursuing an ideology. You're just making your little corner of the world nicer, and along the way adjusting things in near imperceptible ways. A tidy desk, a pleasant interface, a comfortable editor. When it comes to software, there's a difference between making something more efficient because you want to spend less time waiting for it, and making everything faster because speed has become the objective.

I can improve an emulator and still reject acceleration as a philosophy. If I make a tool more responsive so that I can spend a quiet evening reading, drawing, or making something with it, this is a very different relationship with technology than optimizing the technology for the sake of speed. Our kind of degrowth felt good to do, and it's why we did it, but try explaining that sitting in traffic.
