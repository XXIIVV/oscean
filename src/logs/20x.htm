Unintentional Permacomputing

We're visiting family, so some of the choices we make are just bound to turn up in conversations and be questioned. Anything that might look like voluntary simplicity was less an intention than something that naturally followed from higher forces of our environment. I'm sitting in traffic mulling over that "degrowth" that people often think is a choice, which really isn't.

Sure, you can decide not to participate in the upgrade cycle, because you can't afford the extra clutter, or can't afford it literally. There's the obvious degrowth by force when the power goes out or the internet goes down or like software rugpulls. Suddenly, books and typewriters are a more dependable interface that doesn't need permission to work. It's that stuff that everyone confuses with nostalgia.

But what I'm thinking about is the most interesting kind of degrowth, the kind you didn't notice happening at all. You aren't necessarily pursuing an ideology, you're just making your little corner of the world nicer, and along the way adjusting things in near imperceptible ways. A tidy desk, a pleasant interface, a comfortable tool, a few years later you end up somewhere that indicentally looks like degrowth.

If I make a tool more responsive so that I can spend a quiet evening reading, drawing, or making something with it, this is a very different relationship with technology than optimizing the technology for a specific metric. Our kind of degrowth felt natural to do, and it's why we did it, but try explaining that sitting in traffic.
